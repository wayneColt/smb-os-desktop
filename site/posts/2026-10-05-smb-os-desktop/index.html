SMB OS Desktop 0.1: business intelligence that runs on your computer, and answers to you

2026-10-05 · SMB OS Desktop 0.1.0 is out

Business intelligence that runs on your computer, and answers to you. SMB OS Desktop 0.1.0 is out: one download for the owner of a multi-location franchise. Agents on your own computer prepare the morning numbers for every location, check payroll against the schedule, reconcile the bank, draft reminders for late invoices and do the franchise rollup.

1 · Two planes, one authority

You hold the authority; the agents hold none. They read, reconcile, prepare and draft. Anything that would move money, send something outside or can't be undone becomes a proposal bound to one exact action by its SHA-256 fingerprint. It runs only after you type APPROVE, and it runs once. Change one detail and it is a new proposal. An agent can propose; it cannot approve.

2 · Every step leaves a receipt

Runs, proposals, approvals and declines are appended to one file, each line carrying the hash of the line before it. Edit or delete an earlier line and the chain breaks, and the Receipts window says so. It detects edits; it doesn't stop someone who can rewrite the whole file, and the page says that too.

3 · Where the owner works

Today that is your computer: this download, with the data in a folder on your machine and nothing sent anywhere unless you turn on an AI model. The same design on a phone, with approvals within thumb reach, and on a web desk comes next. Neither is in this release.

4 · What 0.1.0 is not

  • Not connected to your systems. It runs on two fictional demo businesses, an auto service franchise and a home care franchise.
  • Not sending anything. An approved payroll or reminder is written to an outbox folder and contacts nothing. The gate is enforced by the program on your computer.
  • Not multi-user. There are no accounts; anyone who can use the computer can approve. Lock session stops agents and approvals; it is not a password.
  • Not signed yet. A browser download meets a first-run prompt on macOS and Windows; the terminal installers check the file's SHA-256 instead.

That list is part of the release, not an apology for it: a tool that can't say what it hasn't done is a tool whose claims you can't check either.
